I India's Big Four — Species Guide

India's medically significant snakebite deaths are overwhelmingly caused by four species — the Big Four. Understanding them is critical because your NLP module infers probable species from symptoms without requiring an image.

Indian Cobra
Naja naja
Neurotoxic + Cytotoxic
  • Hood spreads when threatened
  • Active day & night
  • Venom: post-synaptic neurotoxins + cytotoxins
  • Local: severe swelling, necrosis, tissue death
  • Systemic: ptosis, dysphagia, respiratory paralysis
  • Onset: 1–4 hours
  • Habitat: fields, near villages, paddy areas
  • AP/Telangana prevalence: Very High
Common Krait
Bungarus caeruleus
Purely Neurotoxic
  • Night-biting — most bites occur while sleeping
  • Bite is nearly painless — victim may not wake up
  • Minimal local swelling (unlike cobra)
  • Venom: pre-synaptic neurotoxins (irreversible)
  • Symptoms: drooping eyelids, difficulty swallowing, paralysis
  • Onset: 1–6 hours, often delayed
  • Deadliest of the Big Four — antivenom less effective
  • AP/Telangana prevalence: High
Russell's Viper
Daboia russelii
Hemotoxic + Cytotoxic
  • Loud hiss; coils and strikes rapidly
  • Responsible for most snakebite deaths in India
  • Venom: coagulation disruption, internal bleeding
  • Local: massive swelling, severe pain, blister formation
  • Systemic: bleeding gums, haematuria (blood in urine), DIC
  • Can cause acute kidney failure
  • Onset: immediate to 30 minutes
  • AP/Telangana prevalence: Very High
Saw-Scaled Viper
Echis carinatus
Hemotoxic
  • Small (30–80 cm), fast, aggressive
  • Distinctive rasping sound from scales
  • Venom: anticoagulant, prevents blood clotting
  • Local: pain, swelling, blister
  • Systemic: uncontrolled bleeding from all orifices
  • DIC — Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation
  • Common in dry, sandy, rocky areas
  • AP/Telangana prevalence: Moderate-High

II Venom Types & Mechanisms

Venom is a complex cocktail of proteins, enzymes, and peptides. Understanding the mechanism of each type tells you why certain symptoms appear — and helps you build a better symptom classifier.

Neurotoxic Venom
Attacks the nervous system. Two subtypes:

Post-synaptic (Cobra): blocks acetylcholine receptors → muscles can't receive signals → paralysis. Antivenom can reverse this.

Pre-synaptic (Krait): destroys the nerve terminal itself → acetylcholine can never be released → permanent nerve damage. Antivenom is much less effective here. Time is everything.
Hemotoxic Venom
Attacks the blood and blood vessels. Two pathways:

Coagulopathic (Russell's Viper): activates/depletes clotting factors → DIC → blood can't clot anywhere → patient bleeds from every wound.

Anticoagulant (Saw-Scaled Viper): directly prevents fibrin formation → bleeding doesn't stop.
Cytotoxic Venom
Destroys cells and tissue directly. Phospholipases and proteases break down cell membranes at and near the bite site.

Results in: necrosis (tissue death), severe local swelling, blisters, gangrene if untreated. Can require surgical debridement or amputation in severe cases.
Myotoxic Venom
Breaks down muscle tissue (rhabdomyolysis). Seen in sea snakes and some kraits. Releases myoglobin → kidney damage. Symptom: dark/brown urine (myoglobinuria).

Dry Bite

A dry bite is a venomous snake bite where no venom is injected. Accounts for 20–30% of all venomous snake bites. The victim feels pain and sees fang marks but has no systemic symptoms. Critical for your dataset — "bitten, only pain, no other symptoms" maps to unknown / LOW urgency but still requires hospital observation.

Important for NLP
A victim reporting only local pain with no systemic symptoms could be a dry bite OR very early envenomation. Your model should classify this as unknown venom type and MEDIUM urgency — never dismiss it as LOW.

IV Snakebite First Aid — Right & Wrong

This section directly powers H-M2 (Harmful Practice Detection). These are the dangerous practices your system must detect and correct in real time.

Harmful Practices — Detect & Correct

Tourniquet Application
Tying rope/cloth tightly above the bite. Extremely dangerous. Concentrates venom, causes tissue death from oxygen deprivation, leads to amputation. Most common harmful practice in rural India. Phrases: "we tied his hand", "bandage laga diya", "గట్టిగా కట్టారు"
Incision & Suction
Cutting the bite and sucking out venom. Proven ineffective and harmful. Introduces infection, causes bleeding in hemotoxic envenomation, wastes critical time. Phrases: "cut kiya", "కోసారు", "sucked the wound"
Traditional Healer / Ojha First
Going to a faith healer or herbalist before the hospital. Causes life-threatening delay. Each hour of delay increases mortality significantly. Phrases: "ojha ke paas gaye", "పూజారి దగ్గరికి వెళ్ళారు", "mantra treatment"
Herbal Application
Applying herbs, mud, turmeric, or chilli to the bite wound. Introduces infection, can accelerate necrosis. Phrases: "haldi lagaya", "పసుపు వేశారు", "neem paste"
Alcohol / Electric Shock
Giving alcohol to the patient or applying electric shock to the bite. Both are dangerous myths. Alcohol accelerates venom absorption. Electric shock has zero medical evidence and causes burns.
Immersing in Cold Water / Ice
Placing the bitten limb in ice or very cold water. Causes vasoconstriction → concentrates venom locally → accelerates necrosis.

Correct First Aid — What the System Should Advise

RICE Protocol for Snakebite
Rest — Keep the victim still, minimise movement to slow venom spread
Immobilise — Splint the bitten limb like a fracture, keep it at or below heart level
Calm — Reduce heart rate → reduces venom spread rate
Evacuate — Get to the nearest hospital with antivenom. Immediately.

V Antivenom — How It Works

Antivenom (also: antivenin, anti-snake venom / ASV) is the only proven treatment for snakebite envenomation. Your A-M3 routing module must locate and route to the nearest stocked antivenom center.

How Antivenom Is Made

Snake venom is injected in small, increasing doses into large mammals (horses or sheep). The animal's immune system produces antibodies against the venom proteins. These antibodies are extracted, purified, and lyophilised (freeze-dried) or kept as liquid. When injected into a human patient, the antibodies bind to venom molecules and neutralise them.

Types of Antivenom in India

Polyvalent ASV
Effective against all four Big Four species. This is what Indian government hospitals stock. Made by VINS Bioproducts, Bharat Serums, Premium Serums. Works best within 6 hours of bite.
Monovalent ASV
Targeted against one species. More effective per dose but requires knowing the species. Rarely available in rural settings. Your optional species classifier supports this use case.

Why Cold Chain Matters

Antivenom must be stored at 2–8°C continuously. If it goes above 8°C even briefly, the antibody proteins denature and it becomes ineffective — but looks identical to working antivenom. This is why your IoT cold chain monitoring (H-M3) is clinically critical, not just a tech feature. A patient receiving denatured antivenom will die despite receiving "treatment."

TermMeaning
Vials required10–20 vials for moderate envenomation; up to 50+ for severe Russell's Viper. Each vial = 10 mL.
Administration routeIV (intravenous) infusion only — never IM or SC for ASV
Anaphylaxis risk5–10% of patients have allergic reactions to horse-serum ASV. Adrenaline must be ready.
Antivenom failure (Krait)Pre-synaptic neurotoxins destroy nerve terminals before antivenom can act. Ventilator support is often needed regardless.

VI Epidemiology — India & AP/Telangana

These numbers justify why your research exists. They are your introduction and motivation section.

Global Burden
~2.7 million envenomations globally per year. ~138,000 deaths. 400,000 permanent disabilities. WHO classifies snakebite as a Neglected Tropical Disease (NTD) since 2017.
India's Burden
India has the highest snakebite death rate globally — estimated 58,000 deaths per year (Lancet 2020). This is 50% of global deaths. 1.2 million deaths over 20 years.
AP & Telangana
Combined, both states account for ~10–12% of India's snakebite deaths. High incidence in Krishna, Guntur, Khammam, Warangal districts. Paddy farming belt = high exposure.
Why People Die
Not venom alone. Deaths occur due to: delay to hospital (traditional healer first), harmful first aid (tourniquet), antivenom stock-out at PHC, wrong routing (going to the wrong hospital). These are all addressable by your system.

The "Three Delays" Model

Borrowed from maternal mortality research, the Three Delays model explains preventable snakebite deaths perfectly and is a strong framework for your paper:

DelayWhat It IsYour System Addresses It
Delay 1Decision delay — going to traditional healer first, not recognising severityH-M2 harmful practice detection + urgency scoring
Delay 2Transport delay — no vehicle, doesn't know where to goA-M3 hospital routing + GPS navigation
Delay 3Treatment delay — hospital doesn't have antivenom stockedH-M3 cold chain + stock monitoring dashboard
Paper Angle
Your project directly attacks all three delays in the AP/Telangana context. That is a complete, original, clinically grounded contribution — not just a snake classifier.

VII NLP Terminology for This Project
Natural Language Processing (NLP)
The field of AI that deals with understanding and generating human language. Your project uses NLP to understand symptom descriptions spoken or typed in Telugu, Hindi, English, Tamil, Kannada.
Tokenisation
Breaking text into smaller units (tokens). A token can be a word, subword, or character. MuRIL uses WordPiece tokenisation — it splits unknown words into known subword pieces, which handles Indic scripts well.
Embeddings
Converting words/sentences into numerical vectors (lists of numbers). Words with similar meaning have vectors that are mathematically close. MuRIL produces 768-dimensional embeddings for each input sentence.
Multilingual NLP
NLP that works across multiple languages using a single model. MuRIL is trained on 17 Indian languages so it learns shared semantic space — "drooping eyes" and "కళ్ళు మూతపడుతున్నాయి" are close in vector space.
Intent Classification
Determining the meaning/intent of an utterance. In your case: given a symptom description, classify its venom type and urgency. This is the core NLP task of A-M1.
Named Entity Recognition (NER)
Identifying specific entities in text — names, places, times, medical terms. Your system uses NER-like extraction to identify symptom entities (e.g., "drooping eyelids", "bleeding gums") from free-form descriptions.
Code-Switching
Mixing languages within a single utterance. Very common in rural India. Example: "doctor saab, aankhein band ho rahi hain aur మింగలేకపోతున్నాను". MuRIL handles this natively.
Transliteration
Writing one language using another script. Telugu written in Roman script: "kallu mutupadutunnaayi" instead of "కళ్ళు మూతపడుతున్నాయి". Very common in SMS/voice-to-text. Your dataset must include transliterated samples.
Stop Words
Common words that carry little meaning ("the", "is", "and"). MuRIL handles this internally. You don't need to manually remove stop words — transformer models learn to ignore them contextually.
Lemmatisation / Stemming
Reducing words to base form. "drooping" → "droop". Relevant for classical NLP pipelines. With MuRIL/transformers you do NOT need to do this — the model handles morphological variation.
Attention Mechanism
The core of transformer models. Allows the model to weigh how much each word should "attend to" every other word. "I can't breathe" — "breathe" gets high attention weight as the key symptom word.
Speech-to-Text (STT)
Converting spoken audio to text. Hasini's H-M1 module uses Vosk for offline Telugu STT. The output text of STT becomes the input to your A-M1 NLP classifier.

VIII ML & Model Terminology
Fine-Tuning
Taking a pre-trained model (MuRIL) and training it further on your specific task (symptom classification). The model already knows language — you teach it your specific categories. Requires far less data than training from scratch.
Transfer Learning
Using knowledge from one task/domain in a different task. MuRIL was trained on Wikipedia/web text → you transfer that language knowledge to medical symptom classification.
Multi-task Learning
Training one model to solve multiple tasks simultaneously. Your model has two heads — one for venom type, one for urgency — trained together. They share the same MuRIL backbone, which improves both tasks by sharing features.
Classification Head
A small neural network layer added on top of MuRIL's output. Takes the 768-dim embedding and maps it to your class labels (neurotoxic / hemotoxic / cytotoxic / unknown). You train only this layer aggressively.
Dropout
During training, randomly "drop" (zero out) a fraction of neurons. Prevents the model from memorising training data. You use dropout = 0.3 on your classification head to prevent overfitting on the 5,000-row dataset.
Overfitting
Model memorises training data but fails on new data. Symptom: very high train accuracy, much lower validation accuracy. Prevented by dropout, early stopping, and dataset augmentation.
Early Stopping
Stops training when validation loss stops improving. Patience = 3 means: if validation loss doesn't improve for 3 consecutive epochs, stop training and keep the best model.
Epoch
One complete pass through the entire training dataset. With 10 epochs, the model sees every training sample 10 times, learning patterns gradually.
Batch Size
Number of samples processed before the model updates its weights. Batch size = 16 means process 16 samples → compute loss → update weights. Smaller = more updates per epoch = slower but better on small data.
Learning Rate
How large a weight update step to take. Too high = unstable training. Too low = very slow. For BERT fine-tuning, 2e-5 (0.00002) is the standard starting point.
Logits
Raw unnormalised scores output by the classification head before softmax. E.g., [2.3, 0.1, -0.5, 1.2] for four classes. Softmax converts these to probabilities that sum to 1.
Softmax
Function that converts logits to probabilities. [2.3, 0.1, -0.5, 1.2] → [0.68, 0.07, 0.04, 0.21]. The highest probability = predicted class. The highest value = model confidence.
Cross-Entropy Loss
The training loss function for classification. Measures how far the model's predicted probability is from the true label. Model minimises this loss by adjusting weights during training.
Warmup Steps
Gradually increase learning rate from 0 to the target during the first N steps. Prevents large weight updates at the start of fine-tuning (when the classifier head is random). Standard practice for BERT-family models.

IX MuRIL & Transformer Concepts

MuRIL (Multilingual Representations for Indian Languages) is a BERT-based model developed by Google Research India, specifically trained on 17 Indian languages and their transliterations.

Key Transformer Concepts

BERT Architecture
Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers. Reads text in both directions simultaneously (left-to-right AND right-to-left), understanding context from both sides. MuRIL is built on BERT.
WordPiece Tokenisation
BERT's tokeniser. Splits unknown words into known subword pieces. "మింగలేకపోతున్నాను" is split into subword units. Handles agglutinative languages (Telugu is agglutinative) well.
[CLS] Token
Special token added at the start of every input. After processing, the [CLS] embedding represents the whole sentence's meaning. This is what you feed into your classification head — the 768-dim vector at position [CLS].
Self-Attention Layers
MuRIL has 12 transformer layers, each with multi-head self-attention. Each layer refines word representations using context from all other words. By layer 12, each token has a rich contextual embedding.
Hidden Size
MuRIL base has hidden size = 768. Every token is represented as a 768-dimensional vector after processing. This is the size of the embedding you extract from [CLS] for classification.
Hugging Face Transformers
The Python library you use to load and fine-tune MuRIL. from transformers import AutoTokenizer, AutoModel. Handles all the model architecture, tokenisation, and training loop abstractions.

X IoT & Edge Computing Terms
Edge Computing
Processing data on the local device (Raspberry Pi) rather than sending to a cloud server. Critical for rural India where internet is unreliable. Your system must work fully offline — edge AI.
Raspberry Pi 4
The main compute device. 4GB RAM, ARM Cortex-A72 CPU. Runs your MuRIL inference (quantised), STT (Vosk), TTS, GPS routing, and dashboard. ~$45 device.
TFLite (TensorFlow Lite)
Optimised ML inference framework for edge devices. Hasini's MobileNetV2 image model is converted to TFLite for Raspberry Pi deployment. 3–5× faster than full TensorFlow on Pi.
Quantisation
Reducing model precision from 32-bit float to 8-bit integer. Shrinks model size by 4× and speeds up inference 2–4×. Used to make MuRIL fit on Raspberry Pi RAM and run fast enough.
PIR Sensor
Passive Infrared sensor — detects motion/heat signatures. Used for snake activity detection in your system. Snakes are ectothermic (cold-blooded) — their IR signature differs from background at certain temperatures.
DS18B20
Digital temperature sensor. Used for antivenom cold chain monitoring. Waterproof, accurate to ±0.5°C. Communicates via 1-Wire protocol to Raspberry Pi GPIO.
Neo-6M GPS Module
UART GPS module. Outputs NMEA sentences with latitude/longitude. Used for patient location reporting and hospital routing calculations in A-M3.
MQTT Protocol
Lightweight messaging protocol for IoT. Publish-Subscribe model. Sensor nodes publish data to topics; the Pi subscribes and processes. Designed for low-bandwidth, unreliable networks.
GPIO (General Purpose I/O)
The physical pins on Raspberry Pi used to connect sensors. Pi 4 has 40 GPIO pins. Sensors like DS18B20, PIR, and vibration sensors connect here.
Latency
Time from input to output. Critical metric for your system. Target: <3 seconds from symptom input to urgency output on Pi. MuRIL quantised should achieve ~1–2 seconds inference on Pi 4.

XI Dataset & Evaluation Terminology
Train / Validation / Test Split
Dividing dataset into three parts. Train (80%): model learns from this. Validation (10%): tune hyperparameters. Test (10%): final evaluation on unseen data. Never touch test set during training.
Stratified Split
Splitting such that each split has the same class distribution as the full dataset. Essential for imbalanced datasets. Prevents all "unknown" samples ending up in test set.
Precision
Of all the times the model predicted "neurotoxic", what fraction was actually neurotoxic? High precision = few false alarms. Formula: TP / (TP + FP)
Recall (Sensitivity)
Of all actual neurotoxic cases, what fraction did the model correctly identify? High recall = few missed cases. For medical systems, recall is more critical than precision. Formula: TP / (TP + FN)
F1 Score ← Your Primary Metric
Harmonic mean of precision and recall. Balances both. Best metric for imbalanced classes. Formula: 2 × (P × R) / (P + R). Report macro-F1 (average across all classes equally) in your paper.
Confusion Matrix
Table showing predicted vs actual class for every sample. Diagonal = correct predictions. Off-diagonal = errors. Tells you exactly which classes your model confuses (e.g., cytotoxic confused with unknown).
Ablation Study
Removing one feature/component at a time to measure its contribution. Your ablation: Model A (symptoms only) vs B (+ language) vs C (+ time since bite). Proves each component adds value. This is a paper section.
Baseline Model
The simplest possible model you compare against. Your baselines: random classifier, keyword-matching rule system, TF-IDF + logistic regression. MuRIL must outperform all baselines to justify its use.
Data Augmentation
Creating new training samples from existing ones. Your techniques: synonym replacement, back-translation, random deletion, code-switching insertion. Increases effective dataset size without manual labelling.
Class Imbalance
When some classes have far more samples than others. Symptom: model predicts the majority class for everything. Fix: weighted loss function, oversampling minority class (SMOTE), or stratified sampling.
